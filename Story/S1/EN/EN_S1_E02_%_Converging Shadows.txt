The three children stood trembling, trapped among the trees that blocked their way home. The giant insect growled with a sharp hiss, its eyes glowing red like the embers of hell. Their breaths caught, their bodies frozen—until something strange happened.

From the darkness emerged a faceless shadow. It had no face, no form, just a mass of black mist hanging in the air. For a moment, the three of them forgot their fear. They just stared, not knowing whether to run or kneel.

The shadow drifted closer, then without warning, its body split into three. Each fragment dove swiftly, piercing into the chests of Karin, Reza, and Dimas. They screamed in unison, collapsing to the ground. The world around them suddenly trembled, as if the forest itself was holding its breath.

Their bodies burned. Their eyes gleamed. Something was different.

When the insect leapt forward with its scythe-like legs, Reza felt his right hand ignite with a faint blue glow. Instinctively, he swung it, and from his palm burst a gust of wind that shoved the monster backward. Karin stared in disbelief, but when her body moved on its own, her hand transformed into a thin blade of light, like a transparent sword.

Dimas, standing at the front, shouted despite his shaking hands. From his arm emerged a dense black energy shaped like a spear. He leapt forward, driving it into the insect’s leg, making the creature roar in fury.

The battle had begun.

They moved awkwardly, like children playing with new toys for the first time. Their attacks sometimes hit, sometimes nearly struck themselves. Yet fear and desperation made their bodies faster than usual. Karin slashed at the creature’s wings, Reza blasted it backward with bursts of wind, and Dimas stabbed its body again and again with the black spear.

At last, with one final shout, the three combined their clumsy attacks. The insect’s body convulsed violently, then collapsed with a thunderous crash, its wings broken, its eyes extinguished.

Their first victory. But not without a price.

Dimas, who had fought at the front and attacked the most, fell face-first to the ground. His face turned pale, his breathing heavy, before he lost consciousness. Karin screamed, clutching her friend’s body, tears streaming down. Reza knelt beside them, his hands trembling, holding back sobs. They wept together, terrified of losing the friend who had just risked his life.

That night, they sought shelter. Inside the hollow of a massive tree, they found a natural chamber. Karin and Reza blocked the entrance with a large rock, building a small fire with dry branches. The firelight flickered, but it was not enough to chase away the fear.

Outside, the night was suffocating. Unlike the silence of day, the darkness was filled with eerie cries, booming tremors like the earth itself was shaking, and long howls from creatures unknown—beast or something far worse. Karin covered her ears, Reza held Dimas’ unconscious body close. They felt as if heaven and earth were waging war outside.

Morning came, but not peace. They remained hidden, waiting, hoping an adult would come to save them. The first day passed, then the second. No one came. Their supplies were gone, their water skins empty. Their bodies weakened, but hunger forced them out.

Unaware, the world beyond the tree had already changed. The forest was no longer the same forest. Their steps no longer led back to the village. They had crossed dimensions, entering a place stranger, more dangerous.

With trembling hands, they armed themselves with whatever they could—sharp branches, stones that fit in their grip, and the lingering strange energy of the shadow still inside them. They walked slowly, wary, but hunger pushed them forward.

By late afternoon, they found a different place: a grove filled with fruit trees. The fruits were ripe, their colors tempting, as if waiting to be plucked. Karin stepped forward with great hope, saliva dripping at the corner of her lips.

But suddenly—from the bushes emerged massive snakes, slithering slowly, forked tongues flicking out. Their eyes gleamed sharply, blocking the path to the fruit trees.

Karin and Reza froze, their bodies shaking. Behind them, Dimas was just beginning to wake, still weak.

Hope for food turned into a new fear.

And their story? Was far from over.
