Morning arrived. Thick dew hung in the air, veiling the view until the trees seemed adrift in a white fog. The sounds of the survivors’ bustle echoed: some were preparing firewood, some patching tools, some cleaning up the food from yesterday’s hunt.

Karin, Reza, and Dimas stood in front of a modest wood workshop. The blacksmith greeted them with a weary smile, then placed something on the wooden table: three rings and three necklaces, each with a small blue-glowing crystal.

“Wear them,” he said briefly.

As soon as the rings circled their fingers and the necklaces hung around their necks, their bodies trembled violently. A warm aura wrapped around them, their breathing grew lighter. Power surged through them, almost the same as the mysterious energy that had once possessed them in the forbidden forest.

Karin stifled a gasp.
Reza clenched his fist, feeling the new pulse.
Dimas, face serious, whispered, “It’s like… my blood is boiling.”

The blacksmith patted Dimas’s shoulder. “This isn’t pure magic. These tools draw in nature’s energy from around you. It can run out, but it can also be recharged—like a battery.”

The three exchanged looks, their faces full of determination. The mysterious weapons that once felt like a burden now seemed to fuse perfectly with their bodies.

The hunt began.
They were accompanied by four of the best survivors, who also carried new gear reinforced with crystals. The elders had recommended a hunting zone—relatively safe, yet rich in yield.

The morning fog thickened along the path. Cold crept in, piercing the skin. The rhythm of footsteps over damp leaves filled the silence. Now and then, black birds flew low, sending hearts racing faster.

Inside the zone, the hunt was plentiful: a few deer, wild rabbits, even a large jungle fowl. But the forest never gave without a trial.

A loud rustling emerged from the brush. A scaled reptilian monster with sharp teeth appeared, followed by a swarm of human-sized insects.

Battle broke out.

The survivors fought with all their strength, their bodies slick with cold sweat, breath ragged. The crystal weapons helped, but it was still grueling. One survivor—a young man—was nearly caught in the monster’s jaws. But Reza quickly raised his magic staff, and a blue fireball shot forth, scorching the monster’s maw before it could strike.

“STAND! Don’t die a fool in front of me!” Reza shouted, his face twisted in fury mixed with fear.

At last, with the combined strength of the children and the survivors, the monsters fell. Panting, drenched in sweat, but none had died. That day they returned with an abundant hunt.

Evening came. The bonfire blazed again. The smell of roasting meat filled the air once more. The survivors cheered, welcoming the hunters who had all returned alive. Yet amid the celebration, the hunters’ expressions were different. In that zone, they had felt something heavy, oppressive—as though something greater lurked beyond their sight.

At last, the elder survivor arrived. He sat among them, his face cloaked in secrets. But before he could speak, Reza’s voice cut through.

“We’ll still go. Sorry.”

Silence fell. The fire crackled, faces lowered. Karin bit her lip, Dimas stared ahead, Reza stood firm.

The elder let out a long sigh.
“I suspected as much,” he murmured. “Then listen closely. There is something I’ve never told you.”

He pointed to the ground, then gazed at the sky.
“This world… is not just a forest. It’s like a tower of floors. This forest is the lowest level. They say, if one can reach the very top floor… there is a portal that will return us to our original world.”

Karin covered her mouth, Reza’s eyes widened, Dimas fell silent.

The elder continued, “That is why I thank you. Thanks to you, the other survivors now have weapons, now have hope. But your path is still very long… perhaps too long.”

Silence draped over the bonfire. The laughter of the evening feast had vanished.

They realized—the journey they thought was already heavy… was only the beginning.
