The afternoon sun streamed through the gaps in the trees, painting the sky in soft shades of orange. The three of them—Karin, Reza, and Dimas—had slept through the day after their battle the night before.

Cheers filled the air as evening came. The survivors lit a great bonfire in the center of the settlement. Old songs were sung, laughter echoed through the forest, and the aroma of roasted meat drifted everywhere.

Dimas was the first to wake, his eyes lighting up at once. Without hesitation, he grabbed a wooden plate and devoured three slabs of smoked meat at once.
“Ahhh! This… this is heavenly food!” he shouted with his mouth full, grease dripping down his chin. The survivors chuckled at the sight.

Moments later, Karin and Reza stirred awake, lured by the irresistible smell. Their stomachs growled loudly.
“DIMAS! Leave some for us!” Karin snatched the plate from his hands.
Reza gulped down hot soup too quickly and choked, coughing hard. Dimas patted his back, feigning seriousness. “Easy, Za. If you die from soup, you’ll go down as the dumbest legend in this forest.”
Laughter erupted all around, and the feast broke into full joy.

But in the midst of the merriment, an old survivor rose to his feet. His wrinkled face was heavy with sorrow.
“Children… forgive us,” his voice trembled. “We asked you, so young, to bear a burden that even grown men cannot. We… had no choice. We’ve been trapped here too long, and you are our only hope.”

The bright atmosphere fell into silence. The bonfire crackled softly, faces lowered. Karin stared at the ground, Reza clenched his fists. They knew—this was a truth they couldn’t turn away from.

Then Dimas suddenly stood with dramatic flair, holding up a chicken bone like a sword.
“Then let me, the Hero of Roast Chicken, protect you all! With the power of meat coursing through my body, I am invincible!”
He struck a ridiculous pose, grease still dripping from his chin. The survivors burst into laughter. Karin even covered her face with her hand, embarrassed yet relieved. The mood lightened once more.

“Thank you… the three of you,” the old man finally said, this time with a faint smile.

After the feast calmed down, some survivors led them to a simple wooden building. Inside lay rows of prototypes: a sword with glowing blue crystal lines along its blade, a wooden staff tipped with a crystal core, even arrows whose heads shimmered faintly.

One of the craftsmen explained, patting a small crystal.
“These Mana Crystals aren’t just pretty stones. They’re remnants of the ancient war—between sky and earth.” He pointed to a simple mural on the wall. “These crystals hold pure streams of magic. With a vessel of wood or metal, they become the core of weapons. The difference between them and your natural magic is simple… crystals need a container. You don’t. That is why we admire you… and envy you.”

Karin, Reza, and Dimas listened intently. The crystal glowed softly in his hand, almost like it had a heartbeat of its own.

After the long explanation, one of the survivors made a surprising offer.
“Why don’t you just stay here?” a young woman said. “This place is safe. There’s food, homes, and all of us. You don’t have to keep wandering…”

The three exchanged glances. Their hearts wavered. After all they’d endured, staying here in warmth and safety sounded like paradise. Yet deep down, they knew their journey was not over. The map still bore an X. Mysteries still awaited.

That night ended with laughter, music, and the aroma of roasted meat.
But beneath the smiles, the three had already made their decision: tomorrow they would join the survivors in a hunt, to repay their kindness… and then continue their journey.
