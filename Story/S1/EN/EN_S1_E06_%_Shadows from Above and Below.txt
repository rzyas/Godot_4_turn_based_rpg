Their steps through the ancient temple no longer made them tremble.
Where once every battle had stolen their breath, now they breathed calmly, as if their bodies had grown used to dancing between life and death.

The insect monsters that used to be so terrifying were now nothing more than casualties of the mysterious weapons they held. Karin slashed with her gleaming golden sword. Reza unleashed blue flames with a natural grace, as if the magic was born from within him. Dimas thrust his black spear, its shadow extending like a root of darkness that consumed its prey.

The battle ended quickly. Silence once again took over the temple.

But among the ruins, they found something far more disturbing than the insects. An ancient painting was carved into a giant stone. The lines were rough, yet they clearly depicted something:

In the sky, giant figures with wings and bright light fought, slashing with glittering weapons.

From the belly of the earth, black, massive, and fanged creatures emerged from cracks in the ground, crawling toward the surface.

A battle between the sky and the underground. Between light and darkness.

Karin stared at the painting with a pale face.
"If this is really real… then this forest… everything we've seen…"
Reza swallowed hard, his eyes trembling. "Let's hope… it's just a legend."
But in their hearts, they knew the premonition was too strong to be a figment of their imagination.

The day grew darker. Their supplies were dwindling. The remains of the roasted rabbit they brought from the dead village were almost gone. They had no choice but to survive.

Then, that thick aura arrived.
The air seemed to thicken, their hearts beat faster, not from exhaustion, but from pure fear. Even their minds seemed to crumble, their bodies trembling without cause.

From a distance, a roar was heard. In an instant, goosebumps covered their skin.
It wasn't an ordinary sound. The roar had a pressure, making their chests feel tight just from hearing it.

Night fell.

They made camp at the top of a collapsed temple building, high enough to watch their surroundings. But it didn't calm them. No one could sleep that night. The small fire they kept alive flickered in the wind, but the cold they felt came from within their own chests.

And then, they saw it.

In the darkness of the forest, the silhouette of a monster. It was the size of a tree. Its body resembled a wolf, but on its head grew a pair of branched deer antlers, reflecting the faint moonlight. Its eyes gleamed red, staring directly at them, as if it knew it was being watched.

The monster just stood there. It didn't approach, it didn't attack. It was stalking.
Its very presence was enough to make Karin cover her mouth to stop from screaming, Reza gripped his staff tightly, and Dimas held his breath as if every movement could provoke disaster.

Fortunately, their elevated position served as a protection. They survived the night.
However, the image of the monster's antlers was seared into their minds, preventing their eyes from closing.

In the following days, they survived at the top of the temple, conserving energy, chewing on their remaining food, and drinking the rainwater they collected. Supplies were getting thinner.
But their instincts told them: something was waiting out there.

And that horned silhouette… was still lurking from behind the trees.
