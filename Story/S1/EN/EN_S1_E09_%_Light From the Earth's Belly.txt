That morning, the survivors’ village buzzed with preparation.
An old blacksmith fastened light metal plates onto Karin, Reza, and Dimas, forming simple armor—not heavy, but enough to protect.
On a long wooden table, small pouches filled with herbal mixtures were laid out. “This is an antidote. Drink it before you depart,” said a healer with a grave face.
Other survivors packed dry rations: smoked meat, hard bread, and bottles of water.

The three set out, accompanied by a young survivor named Nilo as their guide. But as they drew closer to the destination, Nilo’s face turned pale. He stopped, refusing to go any further.
“That’s the boundary. I dare not enter… may you return alive.”

The forest grew darker, heavier. No birds sang, only the rustle of leaves. Yet strangely, they did not feel the suffocating pressure they had once faced against the antlered beast. The forest was silent… too silent.

At last, the cave revealed itself.
Its mouth gaped wide, moss-covered walls dripping with cold air. Every detail matched the survivors’ descriptions.

They stepped inside.

At the entrance, insect-like monsters blocked the way, wings buzzing sharp as blades. But for the three of them, this was no longer a challenge. Karin’s golden sword cleaved with a single strike, Reza’s staff unleashed blue fire, and Dimas’ black spear pierced the ground, shadows devouring the swarm. Within minutes, all had fallen.

But their journey was far from over.

Deeper within, venomous snakes slithered along the stone walls. One struck and sank its fangs into Dimas’ arm. Karin cried out in panic, but Dimas only gritted his teeth and steadied himself.
“No effect…” he muttered, breath ragged. The antidote they had drunk beforehand worked, though his body weakened slightly.

Time slipped by quickly. They pressed onward—down rocky paths, through endless tunnels. Only as the sun began to set did a faint blue light shimmer from the depths.

They had reached the heart of the cave.

The sight left them breathless. Massive crystals, glowing blue, jutted from the ground and walls, flooding the cavern with soft light that reflected in every direction. The brilliance sparkled like stars, as if they were standing beneath a night sky.

But the awe lasted only a moment. From a fissure in the rock, a giant serpent lunged, its fangs aimed straight at Karin’s throat.

“LOOK OUT!!”

Dimas moved without thought. His black spear pierced into the serpent’s jaw, stopping it mere inches from Karin. Black blood sprayed, but the danger had only begun.
Hisses echoed through the cavern. From the shadows, monsters emerged one after another—serpents, insects, and twisted abominations, their eyes glowing with rage.

The battle raged through the night.

The blue crystals bore witness to Karin’s flashing golden blade, Reza’s erupting blue flames, and the dark spear that Dimas drove into the ground. Attacks came from all sides. Their bodies were cut and battered, breath ragged, yet they endured. One by one the monsters fell, their blood staining the cavern floor.

At last, as the first light of morning crept into the cave’s mouth, silence returned. No more hissing.
The three stood trembling, covered in dust and blood—but victorious.

The crystal cavern was theirs. No monster remained.

Karin exhaled deeply. “Their information… was right. Without the antidote, we’d be dead yesterday.”
Reza gazed up at the glowing ceiling. “At least now… they’ll be able to live longer.”

As promised, Reza raised his staff. A burst of blue fire shot into the sky from the cave’s mouth—a signal that the battle was over.

Soon after, the survivors arrived, faces beaming with joy. They brought a large cart, ready to harvest the crystals.
Karin, Reza, and Dimas no longer stood tall. They lay asleep atop the cart, not from grievous wounds, but from overwhelming exhaustion.

As their eyes closed, the ringing of iron against crystal filled the cavern—the sound of mining replacing the echoes of battle.

For a moment, the world felt safe.

But… only for a moment.
