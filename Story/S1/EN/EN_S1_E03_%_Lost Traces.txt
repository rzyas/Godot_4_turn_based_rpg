They finally managed to get rid of the snakes. It wasn't because of bravery, but out of desperation and hunger. With their remaining strength, Karin and Reza dragged the still-weak Dimas to a safer area. They were out of breath, their bodies covered in scratches, but at least they were still alive.

Just as despair was about to swallow them, something caught Karin's attention. On the wet ground, faint human footprints were visible. She was stunned, her heart trembling.

"Reza... there's someone else here," she whispered hopefully as she ate a fresh fruit.

After they were full, they rested for a moment. Then, they followed the tracks with shaky steps. The forest seemed to be watching them, the old trees rustling softly as if whispering. The long journey finally led them to a place they had never imagined: a dead village, trapped in the middle of the forest.

The wooden houses stood fragile, their roofs collapsed, doors creaking in the wind. Children's toys were scattered on the ground, pots and household items left as if people had left in a hurry. Yet there were no signs of life. All was silent, only a faint, rotting smell lingered.

Karin hugged herself.

"It's like... everyone just vanished," she murmured.

Reza lifted a rusty hoe from the ground. His hand trembled, not out of fear, but out of anger. "Maybe they met the same fate as us. Trapped here... and never able to return."

That night, they took shelter in one of the dilapidated houses. Karin lit a small fire, Dimas lay weak, and Reza was busy checking the remaining items they could use. From the dead village, they found some tangled ropes, sharp pieces of wood, and some rotten fruits that were still slightly edible. Not much, but enough to survive.

The next day, hunger made them even more desperate. Reza suggested something: creating a trap from the leftover fruits they had found.

"We can't just rely on fruit. We need meat... otherwise, we'll all starve to death."

With their last bit of strength, they set up several simple traps on the edge of the dead village. Karin spread the almost-rotten fruits as bait. Dimas, though his body was still weak, helped by making simple knots. They knew it wasn't a perfect trap, but a fragile hope was still better than nothing.

Dusk fell. The sky turned blood red. They hid behind the ruins of a house, watching the forest with pounding hearts. Only the sound of the wind and their heartbeats could be heard.

Suddenly... a twig snapped.

They held their breath. From the darkness of the trees, a figure slowly emerged. Its body was tall, slender, and graceful. From its head, a pair of gently glowing antlers rose, casting a greenish light that illuminated the surroundings.

Karin covered her mouth, her eyes wide. Reza gripped Dimas's shoulder, who was about to scream.

It wasn't a normal animal.
It was the legendary deer.

The three of them were transfixed, unable to believe that a creature they had only ever known from fairytales now stood before their eyes.

The simple traps had not caught an ordinary animal... but had summoned their last hope.

However, behind that calming light, the surrounding forest felt even darker. It was as if the deer's presence was only the beginning of something much more ominous.
