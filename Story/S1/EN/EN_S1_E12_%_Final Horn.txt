The morning was bright, sunlight piercing through the trees and painting beautiful shadows on the damp earth. Yet the atmosphere was far from ordinary. Cheers and sobs erupted together. Some survivors clung tightly to Karin, Reza, and Dimas, unwilling to let go.

“Don’t die out there!” cried a mother, her eyes swollen with tears.
“We’ll be waiting for you to come back!” shouted the children.

The elder only watched from afar, his face heavy, then gave a deep nod. A worn map was gripped tightly in Reza’s hand, while a blazing fire of determination burned in their hearts. This was the beginning of a journey toward something greater.

The long trek began. A thin mist accompanied them, the forest air eerily still. No monsters attacked along the way. But it was precisely that silence that made their skin crawl—as if something was waiting, something far more dangerous than a mere wild pack.

That feeling returned.
The same dread that had nearly taken their lives before. Their breaths grew heavy, cold sweat dripping down.

Suddenly, the mana crystals in their rings and necklaces glowed brightly, pouring power into their bodies as if aware of the coming danger. The mysterious weapons within them awakened as well, trembling as though demanding vengeance.

Heavy footsteps approached.
A low growl rumbled.

From within the fog emerged a familiar figure—the antlered wolf, the boss monster that had once nearly killed them. Its body was scarred with old wounds, dried blood matting its fur. Yet its eyes still burned with hatred. And this time… it came with a pack.

The battle erupted.

Fangs and claws clashed, the forest fog shredded by the golden arcs of Karin’s blade, the blue flames of Reza, and the pitch-black spear of Dimas. The wolves swarmed mercilessly, their massive bodies falling one by one, drenching the ground in the stench of blood.

The wolf boss roared, its antlers smashing into the ground, cracks spreading like veins. The fight raged fiercely, more brutal than before. Karin was nearly cornered, Reza wounded, and Dimas forced to unleash every ounce of his strength.

At last—with a final combined strike, the three brought the wolf boss down. Its massive body crashed into the earth, its last breath leaving in a bitter howl.

Silence.

The three of them panted heavily, their bodies drenched in sweat and blood. Quickly, they drank the remaining energy potions, their wounds easing slightly though their spirits trembled.

“Not here,” Dimas said sharply, his eyes scanning. “The carcass will draw more.”

With what little strength remained, they pressed on until they reached an ancient ruin.

The ruins were silent, peaceful. No trace of monsters. Only the night sky above, scattered with stars that danced across the heavens. For the first time in a long while, their chests felt lighter.

They sat upon a great stone, kindling a small campfire. Its warmth pushed back the night chill. Karin let out a faint smile, Reza drifted half-asleep, and Dimas gazed at the stars with hollow eyes.

But the peace did not last.

The campfire suddenly went out.
Not by wind.
Not by water.
But by thick, dripping liquid—blood.

They looked up.

And in an instant—
SHARP ANTLERS pierced through Karin’s back. Fresh blood splattered the ground as her body staggered forward.

The wolf boss, with its dying strength, had struck from the shadows, refusing to die alone. Its fading eyes still burned with hatred, as though it would rather perish dragging them down with it.

Reza screamed in hysteria.
Dimas roared, his hands trembling on his weapon.

But before they could act, another shadow loomed behind the wolf. A towering silhouette, radiating an oppressive aura far stronger, stood tall. Their breaths froze.

The wolf boss… had only been a hunting hound.

And what appeared now… was the true enemy.
