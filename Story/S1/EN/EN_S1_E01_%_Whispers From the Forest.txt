In a secluded, small village, trapped by a dark and ancient jungle, three teenage friends grew up together: Karin, a brave and curious 14-year-old girl; Reza, a quiet but clever 15-year-old boy; and Dimas, Reza's peer, who was easily flustered but had a sincere heart.

Since childhood, they had always heard the same prohibition: never step into that forest. Parents, elders, even the village guards would frighten them with terrifying stories—of wandering spirits, bloodthirsty beasts, and people who entered and never returned. The forest was called the Forbidden Forest, and its name alone was enough to make any child tremble just by thinking of it.

But all prohibitions can't always close the door to curiosity. One afternoon, as the sky burned red and the evening wind crept into the cracks of their wooden houses, Karin overheard a whisper from a friend: "The forest isn't as scary as they say. Inside there's a deer with glowing antlers, a legendary creature that can grant any wish."

That news ignited a fire in Karin's heart. That night, in front of a dim fireplace, she shared the secret with her two friends.

"Imagine if it's true," Karin said, her voice trembling, full of hope. "Reza, you could ask for your father to get well. Dimas, you could ask for your family to stop starving. And me? I just want us to be free, to get out of this village. We could live without fear, without rules."

Reza looked down, watching the flickering flames. In his heart, doubt and hope waged war. Dimas gripped his knees, his face pale, but his eyes couldn't hide his curiosity. Slowly, they both nodded. And the decision was made: the next day, they would enter the forest.

The next morning, a chilling air pierced their bones. They brought what little provisions they had: dry bread, a canteen of water, and a small knife. The path to the forest was supposed to be heavily guarded, but for some reason, the guards looked sullen, their eyes weary as if half-asleep. It was as if an unseen force was clearing the way for the three children.

The moment their feet touched the forest floor, the atmosphere changed drastically. Silence. A silence that pressed down on their chests, as if all life was reluctant to stay there. There were no birds, no insects, just the rustle of the wind against the dry leaves. The three of them looked at each other, but no one dared to speak. Their feet moved on their own, deeper and deeper, as if an invisible rope were pulling them.

The sunlight began to vanish, swallowed by the trees. Day felt like night. The air grew heavier, colder, and damper. Their hearts were besieged by fear, but curiosity was stronger than logic.

Suddenly, from behind a bush, an strange sound was heard. It was a harsh scraping, followed by a low, piercing hum. Karin swallowed hard.

"There's... something," she whispered.

The next moment, the bush shook, and a terrifying figure emerged: a giant insect, its body a glossy black, its eyes glowing red. Its legs were long with sharp, scythe-like ends, its antennae twitched wildly, and a hissing sound came from its mouth.

Dimas screamed and ran away in a panic. Reza tried to pull Karin away, though his feet nearly slipped on the slick ground. The creature leaped, stomping its feet on the ground until the earth trembled. The surrounding trees seemed to shake, and leaves rained down.

They ran aimlessly, pushing through the darkness. Their breath came in gasps, their hearts beating uncontrollably. But the deeper they went, the more they realized: the way back was gone. The trees behind them had changed, closing off the path they had just taken. It was as if the forest were alive, swallowing the way out with an unseen, cynical smile.

Fear turned into despair.

In that silence, another sound was heard. Not an insect. It was heavy, slow, yet deadly. It sounded like the footsteps of something much larger, more ancient, and more dangerous than anything they could have ever imagined.

Karin turned to Reza and Dimas, her eyes full of terror but also a fragile determination.

"If we don't find that deer... we'll never get out of here."

Amid the cold rustle of the wind and the dark forest, the three children were unaware: they had just stepped into the jaws of darkness, and the path home had vanished.
