Warmth.
For the first time in what felt like forever, Reza’s body felt warmth. His nose caught the scent of food—not blood, not the damp stench of the forest, but warm soup and roasted meat.

Reza’s eyes opened. He jolted upright, his heart racing.
“Wh… where am I? Did… I die?”

But a familiar voice answered.
“You’re finally awake, Reza.” Karin smiled faintly. Her face looked fresher, though her eyes still carried exhaustion.
Dimas sat beside him, his arm wrapped in bandages, though his expression was no longer as tense as before. “You were unconscious for a long time. I thought you wouldn’t wake up…”

Reza looked at the two of them, tears welling in his eyes. The tears fell freely. They were alive. All three of them were still here.

Dimas then explained everything. Just as they were about to drown, a group of survivors who lived within this forest found them. They were pulled from the raging river, treated, given medicine, and now… given a safe place.

The news was a relief, but also unsettling.
The good news: this camp was the safest zone in the entire forest. The survivors had built a simple settlement, with wooden fences, small fields, and traps for hunting. There was food, there was shelter, and—for the first time—they heard the laughter of other humans.
The bad news: these people had been trapped here for more than ten years. They had never found a way out.

Even so, the atmosphere here was warm.

That night, they sat around a campfire. An old man named Pak Saka served venison soup. A young woman, Lira, chuckled and said,
“If only I had a golden sword like yours, Karin, I would’ve left this forest long ago.”
Another survivor added jokingly, “Or Reza’s staff of fire. I’d be king here if I had that.”
Everyone laughed, and even Reza smiled shyly, though his body still bore healing wounds.

Days passed. Karin and Dimas’ wounds began to heal, and even Reza—who had been on the brink of death—had fully recovered thanks to herbal remedies and the survivors’ years of experience with the forest’s secrets.

But behind that warmth, came a great request.

One night, the survivors gathered. Pak Saka explained: there was a hidden place, marked on no map. A place said to be the lair of the Mana Crystals—glowing stones that radiated pure power. With those crystals, the survivors could forge magical weapons strong enough to protect them.

The problem: none of them were strong enough to even approach it.
Those who tried… never returned.

Their eyes turned to Karin, Reza, and Dimas.
“We know this is asking too much… but only you three can do this.”

At first, they hesitated. Karin gripped her sword tightly. “If the enemy is too strong, we’ll retreat. We can’t promise victory.”
Reza nodded. “We owe you our lives. Because of that… we’ll try. But don’t expect too much.”

The survivors’ faces lit with hope. One of them whispered with conviction, recalling something he had seen.
“I once saw you… from afar. You fought a giant insect at the ancient temple. I knew then… if anyone can claim the Mana Crystal, it’s you three.”

Karin, Reza, and Dimas exchanged glances. Their hearts were heavy, but the bond they now felt with the survivors who had saved them was stronger.
At last, they agreed.

A new mission had begun.
