It was another hot morning, as usual in the barren desert of the second floor. For ordinary people, the sound of birds heralds a peaceful morning. But here, all that could be heard was the distant roar of monsters—that was the morning song of this world.

Reza and Dimas were ready. Their bags were full of dry food, their water pouches were closely guarded, their weapons forged with mana stones. And most importantly—the tribute. The blue and red crystals, the result of years of hunting, collected as payment for entry into the kingdom.

This day was different. They were not alone. They were walking with a group they had known for quite some time—a group of trusted hunters. Among the group was an old man, gray-haired, his gaze filled with experience.

The old man looked at them with heavy eyes.
"Children... are you sure you want to go up to the third floor?"
Reza and Dimas nodded firmly.

The old man took a deep breath.
“You should know. Of all the floors, this second floor is the safest. It's ruled by humans, there's a royal fortress, and there are rules, albeit harsh and cruel. The floor above… only the floor's ruler can move up or down. Don't be crazy. Just stay here.”

Reza stared at the ground. Dimas clenched his fists. They bowed respectfully, but deep down they were firm in their resolve.

However, those words made their chests churn.
Because they remembered. Karin.
Karin's death on the first floor. Her tiny body impaled by horns. Her final smile they would never forget.

And now, they began to put the pieces of the puzzle together.
If only the floor's ruler could move up or down… then someone from the kingdom had allowed—or even caused—Karin's death.

Their blood boiled. Long-buried resentment was now flaring up.
But they held it in.
They had learned: opposing the kingdom now was suicide. They had to be more mature, more patient. There was time for revenge.

The great gates of the kingdom finally opened. A long line of survivors and hunters waited their turn. Their tributes were weighed one by one. When it was Reza and Dimas' turn, the guard announced something.

“The price has gone up. Doubled.”

A small argument ensued. Reza nearly exploded, but Dimas held his shoulder. They had expected something like this to happen. There was no other choice. With grim faces, they handed everything over.

And finally, the iron door opened. They were led by a guard through a long, narrow secret passage.

The passage was lit only by the dim light of candles. The deeper they walked, the colder the air became, and the weight grew heavier. The aura of magical energy pressed against their chests, making their steps heavy.

Until finally, they reached a large door.

The door hadn't been opened by a human—but by the power of dark magic.

Behind the door stood a figure in a black robe. Their face was familiar. The same sly smile. The same eyes. The same voice.

“Hello again, son.”

Reza froze. His blood boiled. His gaze was bloodshot. His breath was ragged.
“You…! You were there…!”

His anger was about to explode. A terrifying murderous aura began to emanate. However, Dimas quickly grabbed his shoulder.
“Reza! Not now. Control yourself.”

It took a moment, but Reza finally came to his senses. He gritted his teeth, lowered his head, holding back his anger.
Deep down, he knew—resisting here would be death.
Better to lose today… than…

The black magician merely chuckled, then raised his hand. The magical door slowly opened. From behind the door, a bone-chilling cold immediately surged in.

A white expanse was visible beyond the gap.
Snow. Icy wind. A foreign world.

Reza and Dimas looked at each other. They stepped forward. Their bodies were immediately struck by a piercing cold, burning their skin.

Third Floor: Snow World.
