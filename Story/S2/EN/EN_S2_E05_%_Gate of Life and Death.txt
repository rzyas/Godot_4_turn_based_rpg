The sound of metal creaked.
The cell gate opened. Two guards entered, their faces blank, emotionless. Roughly, the chains that bound Reza and Dimas's hands were removed, but that didn't mean they were free—they were simply transported to the next stage of suffering.

"Stand up."
The voice was sharp, merciless.

Their bodies were still weak, but they were forced to stand. Their footsteps were heavy and slow, as two guards led them in front, and two others followed behind. Like prisoners being marched to execution.

On either side, the other inmates peered through the bars. Blank stares. Cynical glances. Faces full of despair. No one spoke, no one cared. The silence was more piercing than screams.

Each dead stare pressed on Reza and Dimas's hearts, making their chests tight and deepening their depression.
"Will we end up like them...?" Reza's heart trembled.

Finally, they arrived in front of a large, towering stone gate. It was cold. Closed tightly. Only the faint candlelight on the walls provided illumination. The atmosphere felt like a courtroom, but without justice.

A guard stepped forward, staring at the two men scornfully.
“If you want to survive in this fortress, what will you do?”

The question was brief, but the answer would determine your fate.

Reza and Dimas looked at each other. They remembered what the hunter had said earlier: “You must be useful to this kingdom.”

In hoarse, almost broken voices, they answered together:
“We will do anything to survive.”

They told how they had gotten this far and about their deceased friend, “Karin.”

There was a moment of silence. Then came the loud laughter of the guards.

It was a laughter filled with contempt, a mockery of those who had fallen too low.

“Anything…” he said, one of the guards grinned.
“Even surrendering to slavery, huh?”

Their laughter grew louder.
But Dimas couldn’t take it anymore. When one of the guards mockingly mentioned Karin, his blood boiled.

“Don’t you even mention her name with your rotten mouth!!”

Dimas lunged, despite his weakness. He tried to hit the guard, but his strength wasn't enough. With one push, he was knocked down. The other guards joined in the attack. Reza tried to hold back, but was also beaten, kicked, and stepped on.

Their bodies were battered, their faces bloodied. They were treated like toys.

One of the guards kicked Dimas hard in the stomach, causing him to cough up blood.
"If you can't stand up... there won't be a tomorrow for you."

Slowly, with pain shooting through their bodies, Reza and Dimas tried to get up. Knees shook, hands trembled, but they forced themselves to stand.

Blood dripped onto the floor, heavy breathing burned in their chests. But their eyes were no longer blank—a small, though dim, fire forced them to look ahead.

One of the guards threw two old swords at their feet. Rusty, cracked at the sides, barely worthy of being called weapons. Then came their simple equipment: shabby clothes and worn shoes.

"Take those. If you can survive, even a poor weapon will do. If not... you'll be the next carcasses in the desert."

From behind, a deeper, firmer, colder voice rang out. The leader of the guards finally spoke. His face was stern, his eyes piercing.

“Meet me in seven days at this place. If you’re still alive, we’ll talk again. If not…”
He paused for a moment, then turned around.
“…then you don’t deserve to be in this fortress.”

There were no more words. No mercy.

The great gate began to open. Bright, blinding light poured in, contrasting with the dimness of the room. Reza and Dimas’s eyes closed, momentarily blinded by the scorching desert sun.

“Get out.”

A violent push sent their bodies reeling. They were thrown unceremoniously through the gate, falling onto the hot sand.

With a heavy thud, the steel gate slammed shut behind them. Tight. No turning back.

A moment of silence. Then—
GRAAAAAAARRHHHH!!

A growl boomed from afar. The sand shook. From behind, large silhouettes moved closer. The image of a hungry desert monster, smelling new prey.

Reza gripped his old sword. Dimas reached for another rusty sword, though his hands trembled. Their faces were pale, their bodies battered.

But the choice was gone.
Ahead, the endless desert.
Behind, the ferocious monster drew closer.

They had to survive.
Or die.
