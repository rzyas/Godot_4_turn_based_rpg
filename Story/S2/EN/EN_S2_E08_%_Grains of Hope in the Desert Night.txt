The blue light pulsed slowly, dimming, then growing stronger again. Outside the cave, the growls and eerie chants sounded like a never-ending orchestra of death—as if a monster feast was just about to begin.

Reza and Dimas were still lurking in the crevices of the rocks, their eyes glued to the strange light emanating from the monster's carcass.
"Is this really it...?" Dimas whispered.
"Whatever it is, if it can save us, we have to try," Reza replied firmly.

After a while, they agreed. They would eat first, then extinguish the fire in the cave. If the carcass of their prey was indeed emitting light, then it was no coincidence.

That night, the last of their meat was gone. The small fire they had been tending went out, leaving only a thin layer of smoke that vanished into the darkness.
At first, there was nothing. Silence. Darkness. A silence filled only with the sound of their own still-unsatisfied stomachs.

But a few moments later—"Fwoom..."
The blue light returned. This time, it wasn't just from outside, but from inside the cave. The carcass of their prey, discarded in a corner, slowly began to glow, emitting a shimmer like tiny crystals.

Reza's eyes widened. A smile spread across his face, so wide he couldn't contain it. "Hahahaha! We did it! Dimas, look at this! Look!!" he shouted.

The sound echoed out of the cave.
The large monsters still roaming around immediately turned their heads, long growls shaking the air, and heavy footsteps approached.

Dimas slapped Reza hard on the head. "You idiot! Your voice sounds like a hungry dog ​​barking! Now they know we're here!"
Reza raised his hands, pretending to beg for mercy while holding back laughter. "Hehe... okay, let's have a party too..."

A small glimmer of hope began to emerge. Carefully, they broke apart the glowing monster carcass, sifting through the tiny, hard crystals, pulsing like hearts.

That night they didn't sleep. With rusty weapons in hand, they tried to attach the granules to metal and wood. Strangely, the crystals seemed to merge, seeping in, giving their weapons a faint glow.

Dimas's bone spear now glinted bluish-black, Reza's rusty sword throbbed with a tinge of red and blue. For the first time since being thrown into the desert, they felt something: strength.

Their weary bodies were still riddled with wounds. They finally agreed to rest, so that the day could continue. Although the sound of the "death song" outside the cave grew louder, their hearts warmed by new hope.

Morning approached.
Reza opened his eyes for the first time, his body stiff, his strength still lacking. Dimas woke shortly after, equally weak.

But when they looked outside, their eyes widened.
Dozens of monster carcasses lay around the cave, blood still fresh, flesh torn by the teeth of fellow monsters. Apparently, that night, they had preyed on each other.

"Did you see this?" Dimas could hardly believe it.
"The number... if it's true, they could all become mana stones..." Reza didn't finish his sentence, but the look in his eyes was clear: great power awaited.

Their enthusiasm burned, even though their bodies had not yet fully recovered.

Day came.
Their wounds were slowly healing, the pain still lingering, but they could now move better. With faces covered in dust, sweat, and small smiles, they began scavenging.

One by one, they examined the monster carcasses. And indeed, each one that had died last night left a faint blue glow. There were so many of them that their hands could barely carry them all at once.

“Dimas, this is like a bumper harvest,” Reza said with a chuckle.
“If this keeps up, we could open a mana stone market.” Dimas chimed in sarcastically, but his smile was hard to hide.

They weren't just scavenging; they were also hunting for the remaining small monsters for the day's food. Every step they took was now filled with enthusiasm, unlike the previous two days when there had been only despair.

Night descended forcefully again.
The eerie chanting sounded again—growling, screaming, and footsteps that shook the ground. But this time, from within the small cave, a different sound echoed.

The laughter, small songs, and jokes of two children who had just tasted a small victory.
The smell of roasting meat mixed with the crystal blue light filled the cave, creating a strange contrast between life and death.

In the midst of a monster-filled desert, under a sky full of stars, the two human children felt for the first time… they had a chance to survive.
