Blue flames and black shadows continued to rage.
The ground trembled, and the forest sky seemed to tremble with the roars of the beasts. The fight was no longer just a battle, but a seemingly never-ending war.

Reza roared, his body ablaze with increasingly wild blue flames.
Dimas struck the ground, his spear creating a shadow explosion that devastated the ranks of giant gorillas.

But the monsters continued to emerge. From behind the rubble, from the holes in the ground, from the darkness of the forest. Their numbers seemed limitless.

The two boys' bodies were covered in wounds. Blood poured out. Their breaths were labored. Every muscle in their bodies screamed, refusing to move. But anger and revenge still forced them to stand.

"Kill..." Reza muttered through clenched teeth.
"Destroy..." Dimas whispered, his eyes bloodshot.

They leaped again, slashing, stabbing, and smashing, even though their bodies were already on the verge of collapse.

But monsters also have survival instincts. A sharp bite pierced Reza's shoulder. The gorilla's powerful blow broke several of Dimas's bones. Their bodies were shattered even further.

Hearts filled with fire. Bodies covered in wounds.
The fight became a dance of blood—both beautiful and terrifying.

But slowly, the light in their eyes began to fade.
Their steps became unsteady.
Their weapons' swings grew slower.

Their bodies finally couldn't take it anymore.
Reza collapsed, his chest heaving with agony.
Dimas fell to his knees, his spear slipping from his hand.

"No... don't... I'm not... finished yet..." Reza's voice was hoarse, but he had no strength left to rise.

The monsters surrounded them, ready to deliver a final bite. Both of them were almost out of breath. The world spun, their vision fading.

Then—

The figure appeared.

From behind the crowd of monsters, a figure stood. Tall, clad in a black robe, his face shrouded in shadow. A thick aura of darkness emanated from him.

In an instant, all the rampaging monsters stopped. Their growls turned into submission. The savage eyes now turned to stare at the mysterious figure, as if bowing to a true king.

With a deep voice, echoing like a whisper from hell, the figure stared at Reza and Dimas.
"Another good vessel I've found..."

His hands rose.
Dark magic seeped from his palms, wrapping around the two boys' bodies. Their gaping wounds, their profuse blood, suddenly stopped. Not healed by love, but forced to stop by the power of darkness. Their bodies were preserved, not as possessions, not as human beings.

Reza and Dimas couldn't move. Their bodies seemed frozen, only their eyes still blinking faintly.

Then, as if the world were mocking them, their gaze shifted.

In the distance, where Karin's body had been torn apart and only a faint shadow remained—a face appeared. Karin. But not the gentle Karin. Her expression was now full of anger, full of rejection.

"One of those annoying fragments..." the mysterious figure whispered, his voice low, as if Karin's presence were merely a minor obstacle in his plans.

The bright light repelled the darkness. Karin's body has now completely disappeared, leaving behind her name and fragments of memory.

The darkness is getting thicker.
A magic portal opened in the air, a black vortex with ancient symbols spinning. From there I felt a hot, dry and bitter wind blowing.

Without mercy, Reza and Dimas' bodies were pulled away. Dark magic dragged them along, lifting them onto the back of one of the now completely submissive beast beasts. Like two helpless dolls, they were taken through the portal.

In a matter of seconds, the world changed.

The light of the forest died out, replaced by a sea of ​​sand that stretched as far as the eye could see. The dry desert wind whipped across the face, screaming like the flute of death. The sun was burning, but dark shadows remained hanging in the sky.

They had been moved to the second floor.
A barren world. Wide desert. Monster world.

Here, there is almost no safe place.
And the number of monsters—much more than the first floor.

Reza and Dimas, two boys who lost their best friend, are now even more entangled. They don't know what awaits. Only one thing was certain:
They were no longer ordinary children.
They were pawns in a much larger, dark game.

And the owner of the shadow smiled faintly, satisfied with his new discovery.
