The afternoon felt long. The desert sun burned their skin, while the sound of the wind rustling the grains of sand sounded like a whisper. Reza and Dimas sat at the mouth of the cave, catching their breath, staring at the small pile of mana stones they had collected the night before.

“If all of this can combine with our weapons, maybe we can actually fight that big monster,” Reza said, gripping his rusty sword, which now gleamed faintly.
Dimas chimed in, “Be careful too, don’t let your weapon explode. Your head will be a blue skewer.”
Reza snorted, “Your spear still looks like chicken bones.”

They chuckled, though deep down they both knew: this was the turning point.

As evening approached, they decided to test the power of the mana stones. The monster carcasses still scattered around became their first targets.
Reza swung his sword—srrrtt!—blue light streaked through the air, leaving a luminous scratch in the ground.
Dimas stabbed with his spear—thud!—the ground trembled, and the spearhead sank deeper than usual.

The two of them looked at each other.
“This… is stronger than we thought.”
“If we can control it, we can survive.”

But before they could continue trying, a strange sound emerged. Footsteps… many, coming from the eastern direction of the desert.

From a distance, a large silhouette appeared to be moving. At first, just one, but then five, six, then dozens. Monsters with large bodies, thick hides, bull-like heads with branching horns, walked slowly but with authority.

Reza swallowed. “Uhh… looks like our ‘weapons test’ is getting serious.”
Dimas raised his spear. “If they run, they’ll be faster. So the only way…”
Reza continued, “…is to face them head-on.”

The battle broke out.
Reza charged first, his sword emitting a blue light. The first slash managed to pierce one of the monsters’ neck, black blood spurting. But Reza’s body was thrown back by the force of the impact.

Dimas stabbed from the side, his spear sinking deep, but one of the monsters’ horns nearly tore through his stomach. Reflexively, he rolled over and kicked sand at the monster's face.

“Hah! Try the desert sand, you two-horned thing!” he shouted.

But the monsters were too numerous. They were being pushed back. Their breaths were ragged, and their bodies were starting to become covered in wounds again.
The power of the mana stones was real, but still difficult to control. Every overly powerful attack caused their own bodies to ache, as if the energy hadn't yet fully coalesced.

Until the critical moment arrived.
A large, bull-like monster, twice the size of the others, emerged from behind the herd. Its horns glowed faintly, unlike ordinary monsters. Its eyes glowed a fiery blue.

Reza was stunned. “That… can't be. That monster… has mana stones inside its body?”
Dimas grinned, blood trickling down his temple. “Then the reward is even greater.”

The monster roared, its steps shaking the ground. A single blow from its horns nearly sent Reza flying. Dimas blocked with his spear, but the force of the impact shook his arm to the point of breaking.

“Reza! We have to do it together!”

“Okay, one… two… THREE!!”

Reza leaped upward, his blue sword slicing through the air. Dimas thrust from below with all his might.
ZRAAAGH!!
The sword sliced ​​through the shoulder, the spear through the stomach. Blue light exploded from the monster's body, illuminating the entire desert.

Its body fell with a thud, leaving behind a giant crystal that glowed brighter than anything they had ever seen.

Reza fell to the sand, gasping for breath.
Dimas sat up, laughing despite nearly fainting. “Hahaha… if this keeps up, we won't live long.”

Reza smiled weakly. “But at least… now we know this power is real.”

The two of them approached the crystal. It was large, beautiful, sparkling like a shooting star. But before they could touch it—a strange voice rang out from behind.

“Hmmm… not bad. For kids your age.”

A cold laugh followed. From behind the rocks, several human figures emerged.

Not the survivors who had helped them. Not the friendly hunters.
They were dressed in black, their weapons gleaming, their faces covered with cloth. Their eyes stared at the blue crystal with greedy lust.

Reza and Dimas fell silent.
They realized that their fight had only just begun.
