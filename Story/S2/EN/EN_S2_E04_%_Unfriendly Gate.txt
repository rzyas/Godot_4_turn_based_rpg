Their bodies were limp, helpless. Their breaths were labored, their hands trembling, their lips chapped. Reza and Dimas could only whisper softly, pleading for help.

The hunters who found them immediately summoned a healer from the camp. An old woman in a shabby headscarf arrived with a simple wooden staff. She placed her hand on Reza and Dimas' chests and chanted a spell. A soft light radiated, healing their severe wounds briefly—not completely, just enough to allow them to stand again.

Meanwhile, the leader of the hunting group watched with a cold expression. A tall, muscular man with scars across his face. When Reza and Dimas tried to explain their experience, their voices were filled with fear mixed with despair. But the leader interrupted them with a bored tone.

“Shut up. This isn’t the first time I’ve heard stories like yours. We already know. Enough.”

Reza fell silent. Dimas gritted his teeth, holding back his emotions. They realized that their suffering was only a small part of the greater tragedy that befell so many people in this world.

The healing spell was complete. Their bodies were now healthy enough to walk, though still weak. And suddenly—a loud noise.

Grukkkk…

Both their stomachs growled loudly, demanding food. The group leader turned and tossed a piece of dry bread into their laps.

“Eat. It’s a little, but enough to keep you standing.”

Without thinking, Reza and Dimas devoured the food. Tears fell uncontrollably, mingling with the hard bites of bread. They ate with great gusto, but each mouthful was bitter and full of sadness.

Between eating, another hunter began to explain.
“On ​​this floor, a king rules. You must know one thing: to survive in the fortress, you need a job. There are only three options.”

He raised his hand, counting on his fingers.
“First, the mana crystal mine. Dangerous, harsh, but most needed. Second, becoming a hunter like us—facing desert monsters, searching for food, sometimes battling black magicians. Third… a scientist. But that’s nearly impossible. Only nobles can enter that division.”

Reza and Dimas looked at each other. Their future seemed increasingly uncertain.

Suddenly, the ground shook. From the sandy road, a startling sight appeared: a giant scorpion pulling a cart full of goods. Its body was gleaming black, its pincers as large as a grown man's. But the hunters appeared calm.

“That's our mount. Don't be afraid,” said one of the hunters, patting Dimas on the back.

The cart became their vehicle to the royal fortress.

The journey was short, but every second was filled with terror. Reza and Dimas saw a vast desert inhabited by the shadows of monsters. They also saw other failed groups—bodies dumped in the sand.

Finally, a majestic fortress appeared before their eyes. Black stone walls towered high, giant steel gates stood firmly, guarded by rows of heavily armed soldiers. But the guards' faces were cold, their gazes filled with suspicion of anyone entering.

At the gate, the hunter leader stepped forward. He spoke at length with one of the guarding magicians. After a brief discussion, permission was granted.

Reza and Dimas were disembarked from the cart. They briefly parted ways with the hunters who had saved their lives.
“Thank you…” Dimas’s voice was hoarse, full of sincerity.
The hunters simply nodded, then turned to leave.

The two boys were now led inside by the guards.

Behind the main gate, a shocking sight greeted them.
It wasn’t the splendor of the kingdom they had imagined, but a slum. Dilapidated houses made of wood and cloth, covered in dust and the stench of sweat. Emaciated children sat in the street, and blank-faced adults stared hopelessly.

This fortress may have protected them from monsters… but that didn’t mean it provided a decent life.

Reza and Dimas didn’t have time to say much. The guard who had brought them pushed them roughly, then placed them in a cold, stone cell. The heavy iron door was locked tightly.

“Wait here,” the guard said coldly. “You will be called later.”

Inside the cell, Reza sat limply, his head bowed. Dimas stared at the stone wall, his hands clenched into fists.

“Why is this…? Why is it always like this…”
Dimas’s voice cracked, filled with frustration.

Reza remained silent. Tears fell without her realizing it.
What hurt most wasn't the wounds on her body, but the reality: the power they once possessed, the blue flames and the black shadows, was now completely gone. They were just ordinary humans. Weak. Powerless.

Silence enveloped the room. Only the sound of chains shifting and heavy breathing could be heard.

Until finally, footsteps approached. The iron door creaked open.
A guard stood there with a straight face.

“Follow me.”
