Morning light slowly crept into the stone cave. Reza and Dimas still lay unconscious, their bodies weak, their breathing irregular. They had been asleep for two full days.

Beside them lay a crystal glowing deep blue—the legendary mana stone they had risked their lives to obtain. Right next to it were several bags of dry food and water left by the hunting party that had rescued them earlier.

When they finally awoke, Reza and Dimas stared at the crystal. Their eyes widened, then turned to the food bags. Without hesitation, they devoured them. Salty, bland, and tough—yet to them, it tasted like the most delicious food in the world.

That day, they emerged from their hiding place. The desert was still the same—silent, full of hot sand and sharp rocks. Occasionally, they heard the growl of a monster in the distance, but there was no sign of the hunting party nearby.

Carefully, they retrieved water supplies from the cold crevices of the rocks, then hunted small monsters for additional food. Although their bodies were not yet fully recovered, their survival instincts kept them moving.

As evening approached, they finally met up with the group of hunters who had rescued them.

Laughter erupted.
“Hahaha, you’re finally awake!”
“We thought you were dead, just a corpse in this desert!”

Some members of the group teased them loudly, but their gazes weren’t filled with contempt—more like a family welcoming a little sibling home.

With faces full of gratitude, Reza and Dimas took out the legendary blue mana stone. They bowed deeply.
“This… is for you. As a thank you for saving us.”

But the group leader shook his head slowly.
“You need it more than we do. Keep it.”

To convince them, he showed them the group’s catch. It was no longer a blue crystal, but a reddish-purple mana stone that gleamed intensely. One member even whispered, “The most powerful is gold… but none of us have ever found it.”

Reza and Dimas understood the comparison at a glance. They were stunned. The blue mana stone they had fought so hard for was still far below what these hunters had achieved.

"Then how can we repay all your kindness?" Dimas asked seriously.

The group leader stared intently, then replied coldly.
"Be kind to others. That's the best way to repay someone."

The words were simple, but they left a deep impression.

As the atmosphere began to thaw, Reza dared to ask:
"How do we get to the third floor?"

In an instant, the cheerful atmosphere turned frozen. The smiles vanished, only heavy gazes remained.

"Even with our current strength, we're only halfway to the minimum. Getting to the third floor isn't just about surviving... it means challenging a crueler world."

Reza and Dimas stared at each other blankly. It was heavy. This world seemed endless.
Dimas recounted his seven-day survival mission when they encountered several guards.

The group then chuckled, trying to break the ice. They explained,
“Seven days of survival? Hahaha… you’ve automatically been designated as hunters. When you reach the guard post, you can exchange the monsters you kill or mana stones for weapons, medicine, and even food that’s impossible to find in this desert. If you’re lucky, you might even be summoned into the royal fortress when the king needs you.”

Days passed. Reza and Dimas met with the group more often, sharing supplies, exchanging stories, and even hunting together occasionally. From their initially unfamiliar relationship, they slowly became close.

Time passed imperceptibly.
Days turned into months.
Months turned into years.
Three years had passed.

In those three years, their bodies had changed drastically. Muscles built, gazes became cold, reflexes sharpened. Their weapons were no longer just rusty swords and blunt spears—the mana stones they’d collected over the years had fused with them, making them far more powerful.

Their physiques, techniques, and mentality… had improved tremendously.

In the past year, they had even separated from the group of good hunters. They walked alone, helping other survivors trapped in the barren desert. The words of their former leader—"do good"—were truly dear to them.

That kindness became more valuable than any mana stone.

But finally, the day arrived.
They already knew one important thing: the portal to the third floor could only be opened by the royal mage.

And now, after three years of suffering, they felt strong enough. Very strong, even.

Reza and Dimas stood atop a vast sand dune, gazing out at the horizon, where a large, black shadow loomed—the royal fortress.

They looked at each other, then nodded.
"The time has come."
