Hope seemed to crumble in an instant.
Before Reza and Dimas, the shadow of a terrifying desert monster approached with a long growl. Their eyes widened, their bodies stiff, as if death were ready to take them at any moment. There was no way to fight. No chance.

For a moment, a faint hope arose—perhaps the gate would open again, like a miracle. But from previous events, they knew the answer: no.
The gate never opens for those who have been thrown outside.

“Run away…” Reza’s voice was hoarse, but full of determination.
That was the only answer.

The hot sand burned the soles of their feet. Their breaths were labored, their bodies covered in wounds, and the blazing sun blurred their vision. The desert wasn’t just a stretch of sand—there were sharp rocks, towering cliffs, and the shadows of towering mountains that seemed to surround them.

The monster continued to pursue. The thump of giant footsteps echoed from behind, followed by a roar that shook the air. They ran with all their might, leaping over rocks, falling, and then getting back up again.

Finally, nearly out of breath, they found a narrow gap in the rock wall. With difficulty, they squeezed through, their bodies scraped by the sharp stone, but managed to get deeper.

Behind the gap was a fairly large space—a hidden cave. Dark, damp, but safe for the time being.

Outside, the thumping sounds could still be heard. The monster chasing them waited, circling around the cave entrance, as if aware of its prey within.

Reza and Dimas lay exhausted, breathing heavily, sweat pouring down their faces.
"We... have to survive... seven days," Reza gasped.
"If we don't... it's over."

They looked at each other, and that's when a new resolve emerged. They began formulating a survival plan, even though they had nothing but rusty swords and their remaining strength.

As day passed, the temperature inside the cave remained hot. Their stomachs growled, and their hunger grew. There was no water, only the dampness of the rock walls barely helping.

The monster outside began to recede. Their survival instincts urged them to cautiously exit the cave.
But this time, it wasn't to fight, but to search for food.

As they set foot outside, a strange sound was heard. "Sssskhhrrhh..."
From behind the rocks, a swarm of fire rats emerged. Their bodies were large, their fur like glowing embers, and their sharp teeth belched hot smoke.

They attacked without hesitation.

A fight broke out.
With a rusty sword, Reza slashed, though with difficulty, each blow hurting his own arm. Dimas held a fire rat's bite on his arm, screaming in pain, then stabbed with his sword until it died.

Their bodies were clawed and burned, but finally, the fire rats collapsed, their breaths labored, their bodies covered in fresh wounds.

They looked at each other, and among the fire rat carcasses...
the smell of burning flesh assaulted their nostrils.

Their stomachs churned even more. Their noses and tongues seemed to be begging for food. But reason held them back.
"Don't... we'll die of poisoning," Reza said, though his eyes continued to dart to the carcasses.

Dimas nodded slowly, though hunger was clearly tormenting his mind.

They moved again, searching for something more palatable. From a distance, they spotted a herd of fire sheep. Their bodies were large, their horns glowing red, and their breath emitting hot steam.

Their stomachs growled again, louder this time. Their nearly collapsed bodies seemed to be filled again by the mere hope of food.

“That’s… our target,” whispered Reza.
They knew this was dangerous, but there was no other choice.

The first hunt began.
They hid behind a rock, observing the movements of the fire sheep. Reza gave a signal, and they pounced on one that had strayed.

But the sheep was more ferocious than expected. With its horns, it charged at Dimas, hitting him hard in the chest. Dimas was thrown, hitting the rock, blood spurting from his mouth. His breath was labored, and he felt as if his life was about to fail.

“DIMAS!!!” Reza shouted, desperate.

With his remaining strength, Reza slashed at the sheep’s horns, even though his sword was cracked. Dimas, though nearly unconscious, thrust his sword into the sheep’s stomach with a furious cry.

The fire sheep finally collapsed, its body burning with the last embers before dying.

Dimas lay weak, nearly dead.
Reza dragged the sheep's carcass back to the cave, even though he had to half-drag Dimas' body as well.

In the cave, they both collapsed.
But the smell of burning flesh slowly filled the room.
For the first time in a long time, there was a faint hope—they had managed to bring food.

The first day was over.
But outside, the roars of other desert monsters could still be heard, waiting for their turn to prey.
