The meat roasting over the small fire gave off a tantalizing aroma. The smoke clung to the cave walls, suffocating the air but simultaneously satisfying their hungry stomachs.
Reza took the first bite, hot, stringy, but tasting like the most delicious meal of his life.

They were both silent for a long time, only the sound of chewing could be heard.
But after that, the silence turned to pain.

“If Karin were here…” Dimas’s voice broke, softly.
Reza looked down, his hands trembling as he held the roasted meat. “She would definitely smile, saying we’re stupid for eating half-burnt meat like this.”
A small laugh escaped, but it quickly turned to tears. Tears fell, mixing with sweat and desert dust.
The image of Karin—her smile, her voice, her body impaled by a giant’s horn—was so clear in their minds.

Their home, their village, their former life… it all seemed like a distant dream. Now all that remained was the barren land and the growls of a bloodthirsty monster.

The night was filled with eerie sounds once again. The growls, roars, and grinding of teeth outside the cave sounded like a hellish chant. Every second reminded them that they were mere prey in this land of monsters.

But that fear turned to determination. With their remaining strength, they began gathering whatever tools they could find. Dry wood from the ruins, the bones of small monsters, and sharp stones. With makeshift bindings, they fashioned primitive weapons—simple spears, daggers made of bone, and shields made of ribs bound with burnt hide.

“If we just hide, we’ll surely die. If we fight, at least we have a chance,” Reza said with sharp eyes.
Dimas nodded, though his body still ached.

Morning came. Warm light penetrated the cave’s small opening, illuminating their pale, tired faces. The sounds of the monsters outside slowly died down, leaving only a few still roaming, devouring the carcasses of their own prey.

They seized the opportunity.

They devised a simple plan: go out, face the small monsters, force their bodies to adapt to combat without their shadow powers.

The first battle was difficult. Their muscles weren't used to it, and their old instincts—which had once relied on the power of shadows—made them hesitate. Their swords often misfired, their bodies too slow to dodge.

A small monster leaped at Dimas, nearly sinking its claws into his neck, but Reza blocked it with a wooden spear, which snapped instantly.
Both fell, rolling on the ground, blood spurting from Reza's shoulder.

But despite their injuries, they refused to give up. With furious shrieks, they slashed, stabbed, and finally, the monster collapsed.
The fight continued. Their bodies were covered in fresh wounds, their breaths almost gone, but as the hours passed, their muscles slowly regained their composure. Their movements became more agile, their swings more precise.

Finally, all the small monsters were dead. The desert ground was covered in blood and corpses.

From a distance, several pairs of eyes watched. A group of hunters—the ones who had rescued them from the desert—stood on a rocky hill.

Their leader chuckled. "It seems they've chosen the hard path…" he said, narrowing his eyes.
One of the members chimed in, "Not bad for kids their age."

They didn't dismount, just watched, then left the desert with blank expressions.

It was late afternoon.
Reza and Dimas gathered the spoils of their hunt. Some of the monster meat was used as food, while some of the bones and skin were reprocessed into more powerful primitive weapons.

Their bellies were full, and their energy had returned a little. They walked further, and not far from the battle site, they heard a trickle—the sound of water.

"Water…" Dimas whispered, his eyes sparkling.

They ran, and sure enough, a small spring was there, sparkling clear in the twilight. But as they approached, their eyes caught something.

Beneath the water's surface, a large, vague silhouette moved slowly. It was like something was hiding, waiting for them to let their guard down.

Their bodies tensed. Instinct screamed: danger.
Fortunately, they realized before it was too late. Quickly, they retreated, enduring their agonizing thirst. The silhouette remained still underwater, not emerging, just sitting there as if waiting for its prey to come to them.

“Not now…” Reza muttered, holding back his anger despite the burning in his throat.
But he was still grateful for a small supply of water.

They finally returned to the sheltered cave, though their bodies were weak from being thirsty for most of the day.

That night, the smell of roasting meat filled the cave again. They ate heartily, though their hearts remained wary.
But then, something caught their attention.

The remains of the monster carcass they had discarded that morning—bones, blood, body parts—slowly emitted a blue light.
Not just one, but several other monster carcasses around the desert also began to glow faintly, like gemstones born from death.

Their eyes widened.
“That’s… a?” Reza asked.

Dimas stared in disbelief, then muttered softly:
“Is… that mana stone… a fossil from a monster? Something left over after they died?”

The blue light continued to pulse, growing brighter, as if beckoning them closer.
