The small fire in the center of the circle of tents crackled softly, fighting off the cold that continued to creep in from all directions.
The blizzard outside was beginning to subside, leaving a frozen silence that felt strangely comforting.

Dimas sat leaning against his gear bag, his eyes half-lidded in exhaustion.
Reza stared at the flames, his fingers tapping lightly on the handle of his staff.

Before them, the elite group of mages relaxed—thick blankets wrapped around their bodies, their faces still mostly covered by hoods.
Only their eyes shone in the reflection of the firelight.

[SCENE: A FROZEN SECRET]

One of them, the leader of the group—stared at them silently for a moment.

Then he spoke, quietly but sharply:

“You know… that Frost Titan monster you defeated…
…was impossible for even the two of us to defeat.”

Dimas straightened up, staring back.
Reza frowned, almost laughing contemptuously—but stopped when he saw the solemn expressions of everyone there.

“We tried,” the leader continued, “About a year ago.
Eight of our best men, fully equipped… and we still failed.”

The other mages nodded heavily.
Some of their faces still bore the scars of ice, permanently etched into their skin.

“But you…”
The leader glared at Reza and Dimas.
“…just the two of us. No protection. No backup. And you won.”

Silence fell. The fire crackled softly, the only sound.

[SCENE: ANOMALY]

The mages in the group narrowed their eyes, then turned to the old mage sitting a short distance away, wearing a dark blue crystal necklace.
He nodded slowly.

“I can tell a person’s power… with a single glance.”

The old mage’s eyes flashed faintly.
“And you… you’re not just strong.
You’re an anomaly.”

Dimas and Reza looked at each other.

“An anomaly…?” Reza asked quietly.

“Something that shouldn’t exist,” the old mage replied.
“Somehow, you’re growing too fast… it’s as if the world itself is adjusting to catch up with you.”

The night wind howled outside the tent, as if confirming those words.

[SCENE: REJECTED OFFER]

Dimas gripped his spear.
Reza finally spoke, his voice low but firm.

“Allow us… to join you.”

Several people in the group glanced at each other.
A faint smile appeared on the leader’s face, but it quickly faded.

“We’d love to…” he said quietly, “but we can’t.”

Reza frowned.
“Why?”

“Because we’re on a… secret mission.
A mission that will only succeed… if no one knows we exist.”

The words cut like a cold knife.
Dimas lowered his head. Reza clenched his fists.

“Sorry, boy,” the leader said. “Your path… is not our path.”

[SCENE: FAREWELL AND GIFT]

By morning, the storm had completely passed.
The white sky lay silent as they stood in the snowy fork, facing each other one last time.

The silver mage walked over and held out a small object:
a jet-black crystal, a faint flame pulsing within it like a heart.

“This is a gift.”
“If this glows blue, there’s a human nearby.
If it glows red, it’s a non-human entity… a monster, or worse.
If it’s purple, it’s… both.”

Dimas accepted it slowly.
Reza nodded silently.

“Use it wisely,” the mage said. “And stay alive.”

The wind blew softly, carrying the morning’s first snowflakes.
The group walked away, one by one vanishing in a flash of teleportation.

Leaving behind only footprints… and a long silence.

Dimas stared at the crystal in his hand.
Reza took a deep breath, letting it freeze in the air.

They were alone again.
Just like before.
