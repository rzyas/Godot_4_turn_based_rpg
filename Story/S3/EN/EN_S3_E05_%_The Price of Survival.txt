[SCENE: MARKET SHADOWS BEHIND THE WALL]
They finally made it across the snowfield to the great wall.
From outside, the bustle of people trading could be heard.
From the gap in the tower, Reza and Dimas caught a glimpse: it looked like a market.

Behind the defensive wall were rows of thick cloth tents, ice-wood tables, and fur-clad people selling supplies, medicine, war equipment, and survival gear.
Surprisingly, the prices were much cheaper than what they had seen on the second floor.
Some of the food even seemed… more humane. There was steaming hot soup, black bread, and salted meat.

Dimas frowned.
“So… the idea that the second floor is the safest place… is probably just an empty rumor?”

Reza just snorted,
“As long as I’m alive… that’s enough.”

They chuckled, though their eyes were blank with exhaustion.

[SCENE: WARNING AT THE GATE]
When the sun was directly overhead, they stood before the icy steel gates again.
This time they looked around —
several frozen bodies lay outside the gates.
Some were torn to shreds, others frozen, clutching empty sacks.

The unwritten message was clear:
“No money, no justice.”

A guard on the tower spoke flatly when they asked,
“What do we get for paying tribute?”
The answer was simply:
“A place to survive the night of madness.
And even then, it won’t last forever.
Inside, there are no ordinary people. Don’t expect mercy.”

[SCENE: NEVER-ENDING HUNT]
By late afternoon, they returned from the snowfield, their bodies covered in cuts and bruises.
That day, they had hunted small monsters, fighting almost non-stop.
They had managed to obtain a few mana crystals, but they were still far from enough to pay the tribute.

Their steps were unsteady, their breath frozen in the air.
But now they had one temporary goal: gather enough tribute to enter.

[SCENE: A WARM NIGHT FOR ONCE]
As night fell, they found a small cave, narrow and dark, just enough to shelter them.
Several monsters began to scurry to their own dens—a sign of the approaching snowy night.

Reza and Dimas blocked the cave opening with rocks and lit a small fire.
From the few supplies they had purchased that afternoon, a simple miracle emerged:
a thick, warm blanket, a small stove, and a hot water bag.

For the first time since they had set foot on this floor…
they fell asleep without shivering, their bodies warmly enveloped in blankets.
The blizzard roared outside,
and they slept soundly.
